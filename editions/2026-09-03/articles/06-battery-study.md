A two-year field trial of sodium-ion cells in stationary storage reports
capacity retention above ninety per cent after two thousand full cycles, which
is in line with the laboratory figures and well ahead of what the sceptics
predicted for real installations.

The caveats are the usual ones and the paper is honest about them: a temperate
climate, a conservative charging profile, and a battery that was never asked to
do anything in a hurry. None of that is how a car uses a battery. All of it is
how a house does.

For the shed project, this is the study to cite. It does not change the
decision — the cells are still hard to buy at retail — but it retires the
argument that they will not last.
