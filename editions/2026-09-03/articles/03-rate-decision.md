The central bank left its policy rate unchanged on Wednesday, as almost
everyone expected, and then declined to say much about what happens next,
which rather fewer people expected. The statement was two paragraphs shorter
than August's and dropped the sentence about "the path of further easing"
altogether.

The reaction was the interesting part. Two-year yields rose slightly on the
release and then fell back; the long end moved more. The reading that took hold
by the close was that the bank has stopped promising cuts it may not be able to
deliver, which markets chose to hear as a warning rather than as honesty.

This matters to you in one narrow way: the fixed rate on the savings account
was pegged to the next cut, and the next cut just became less certain. The
Ledger's column argues nothing has really changed. That is probably right, and
it is also what people say on the days when something has.
