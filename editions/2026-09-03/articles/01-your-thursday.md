The Henley review is at half past nine, on a call, and it is the only thing on
the calendar that other people are relying on you for. The notes from last
week's session are in the project folder; the open question was whether the
second phase starts in October or waits for their audit, and nobody has
answered it since. Expect to be asked.

After that the morning is yours until the dentist at half past two, which is a
cleaning and not the crown — that was rescheduled to the twenty-second, and the
reminder has been moved to match. The practice is on Pennsylvania Avenue by
Eastern Market, and the Orange Line is single-tracking through Eastern Market
until the seventeenth. Walk it. From the house it is eighteen minutes down
East Capitol, which is quicker than the shuttle bus and considerably calmer.

> The only thing on the calendar that other people are relying on you for is
> at half past nine. Everything after that is negotiable.

Weather is the other fixed point. Ninety-one and humid by two, cloud building
from the west, and the chance of a thunderstorm climbs past even odds a little
after five. If the walk is happening today it should happen before the
dentist, not after. The umbrella is by the door, where it has been since
Tuesday, unused.

Three emails need a reply and none of them is urgent; they are on page two.
The landlord's is the one to do first, only because it has been sitting longest
and his tone will not improve with age.

Nell arrives on Saturday at eleven, by train, into Union Station. Saturday
looks clear after a wet Friday night, which is the case for Great Falls in the
weekend pages rather than the museum. The Commanders open at home on Sunday at
one; the sports pages have the shape of it.

Nothing else has a time attached. The rest of this paper is what happened in
the world while you slept, filtered for the parts you have said you care
about, and a few things from your own notes that seemed worth seeing again.
