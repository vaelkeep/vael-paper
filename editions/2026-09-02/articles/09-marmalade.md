The photograph was taken at four in the afternoon, which is the hour at which
nothing whatever is required of him, and he knows it.

It is worth being clear about what is happening here, because the picture
invites a misreading. This is not a roar, or a complaint, or a warning. It is a
yawn — the ordinary involuntary kind, caught at its widest by a shutter that
happened to be open at the moment of maximum commitment. Photography does this.
It takes a continuous event, freezes one hundredth of it, and hands you back
something that looks like an opinion.

> The camera is very good at making a passing state look like a settled
> position. So is the news.

The deck boards are worth noting too. They run north to south, they hold the
warmth of the afternoon about twenty minutes longer than the grass does, and he
has established by patient experiment that the third board from the step is the
warmest of them. He arrives at it without deliberation, the way you find your
own chair in a dark room.

There is no news in any of this. That is rather the point of including it. A
paper assembled by a machine overnight will fill itself, given the chance, with
things that have consequences — rate paths, orbital debris, the failure modes of
unattended systems — and a reader who takes only that will finish every edition
slightly worse off than they started.

So: the third board from the step, at four in the afternoon, with the light
coming in low along the grain. Nothing follows from it. He is entirely well, he
has not been wronged, and he would like it understood that he was, in fact,
merely yawning.
