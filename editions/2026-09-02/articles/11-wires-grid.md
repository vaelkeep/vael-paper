Three separate assessments published this week reach the same conclusion by
different routes: reserve margins across two large interconnections are thinner
going into this winter than in any of the previous four, and the reason is less
about generation retirements than about how quickly demand has grown.

The regulator's own filing puts the margin at eleven per cent against a target
of fifteen. The trade body's assessment, which uses a stricter definition of
firm capacity, puts it lower still. Neither expects an outage under normal
conditions; both note that "normal conditions" is doing a great deal of work in
that sentence.

What has changed is the shape of the load. Datacentre demand does not vary with
temperature, so it does not fall away in the hours when heating demand peaks —
it stacks on top. A system planned around a winter evening peak that lasts three
hours now faces one that starts higher and stays there.

The mitigations available before December are all demand-side, and all of them
have been used before: interruptible contracts, voluntary curtailment, and
appeals to the public. The supply-side answers take years, which is the point
the assessments are actually making.
